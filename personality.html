Personality

Traits and Hobbies

  • Widely Curious

I like to understand how things work. In school, this helps me ask questions and learn beyond just memorizing information. It is especially useful in Computing because I need to understand how programs and websites work.

  • Very Persistent

I do not always understand something the first time. Instead of giving up, I try different ways of understanding it. This helps me continue working through difficult assignments and courses.

  • Loves Drawing

Drawing is one of my main hobbies. It allows me to be creative and gives me a break from schoolwork. Drawing also helps me develop creativity and imagination, which can be useful when solving problems and developing ideas.

Hero

Robert J. Hanlon

Robert J. Hanlon was an American writer known for the saying called Hanlon's Razor. His quote, "Never attribute to malice what can be attributed to incompetence," became associated with him through Murphy's Law Book Two, published in 1980.

I look up to him because this quote reminds me not to assume that people have bad intentions when they may have simply made a mistake. I try to apply this idea in school and everyday life by understanding a situation before judging someone. It helps me stay calm, avoid unnecessary arguments, and focus on solving problems.

LLMs as a Tool, Not a Crutch

Artificial intelligence has become a common part of education, especially with tools like ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot. As a Computing student, I believe that using LLMs as a tool and not a crutch means that AI should help me learn instead of doing the work for me. AI can explain difficult topics, help me find mistakes and give me ideas, but I should still be able to do my schoolwork on my own. How I use AI determines whether it helps me learn or stops me from learning.

One way I can use AI is as a personal tutor. For example, when learning object-oriented programming, I can ask ChatGPT to explain classes, objects and constructors in simple terms. If I am working on a Java Cow class with an ID, breed, age and weight, I can ask AI to explain how the class works instead of asking it to write the whole program for me. I can also ask it to give me similar questions and try to solve them myself. A 2025 Inside Higher Ed survey found that 50 percent of students who used generative AI for coursework used it to ask questions like a tutor, while 46 percent used it to study for tests or quizzes. This shows that AI can be used to support learning instead of just completing assignments.

AI can also help me find mistakes, but I still need to understand the answer. For example, when doing physics problems about thermal radiation, I could compare my work with an AI explanation and realize that I forgot to change Celsius to Kelvin before using the Stefan-Boltzmann equation. In web programming, if my CSS is not working on GitHub Pages, I can use AI to help me check my stylesheet path or find a mistake in my HTML or CSS. However, I still need to test the solution and understand why it works. Brett Leonard's AI as a Tool, Not a Crutch presentation explains that AI is only one tool and that students should understand its strengths and limits. He also explains that AI can make work faster without always making it better. Getting an answer quickly does not mean that I actually learned the material.

Using AI too much can also affect my ability to think and work on my own. If I use ChatGPT to solve every programming problem, I might finish my assignment but still not know how to write the code during a test. The same applies to physics. If AI does every calculation for me, I may not learn how to choose the right formula or solve the problem myself. The Inside Higher Ed survey found that 55 percent of students who used AI for coursework reported mixed effects on their learning and critical-thinking skills. This means AI can help in some situations but can also cause students to think less. Because of this, I should try problems myself first, use AI when I get stuck, and then practise the solution on my own.

In conclusion, LLMs can be useful for my education if I stay in control of my learning. I can use AI to explain OOP concepts, check my physics work, fix HTML and CSS problems, create practice questions and develop ideas. However, I should not let it do all my assignments or solve every problem for me. AI has useful strengths, but it also has limits. For me, using AI as a tool means using it to make learning easier while still building the knowledge and skills I need to work on my own.
